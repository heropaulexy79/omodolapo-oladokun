Digital Development in Africa

How has digital development changed across selected African countries, where do major gaps remain and which infrastructure differences deserve greater attention?

01 · The problem

Digital access is not one metric.

Internet usage is shaped by several layers of infrastructure. A country may record high mobile-subscription levels while actual internet adoption, fixed broadband or secure internet capacity remains weak. The project therefore examined five related indicators rather than reducing digital development to a single ranking.

02 · The approach

From a broad global dataset to a focused analytical model.

  1. Selected five World Bank WDI indicators covering adoption, connectivity and enabling infrastructure.
  2. Filtered the global data to 11 selected African countries and the 2010–2024 period.
  3. Unpivoted yearly columns into a long format suitable for time-series analysis.
  4. Retained unavailable observations as null rather than falsely treating them as zero.
  5. Created DAX measures for 2024 comparisons and designed a one-page executive dashboard.

03 · What was analysed

Adoption, infrastructure and the gaps between them.

Internet adoption over time

Country-level trends were compared across 2010–2024 to show both the direction and pace of adoption.

Mobile connectivity versus actual usage

The comparison tested whether widespread mobile subscriptions consistently translated into internet participation.

Electricity and secure infrastructure

Electricity access and secure-server capacity were assessed as enabling conditions—not as interchangeable measures of digital participation.

Interpretive disciplineThe dashboard is designed to surface relationships and gaps. It does not claim that correlation between infrastructure indicators proves causation.

04 · Decision value

Different gaps require different interventions.

The analysis helps distinguish an adoption problem from an infrastructure problem. A market with strong mobile penetration but weaker internet use may require affordability, digital-literacy or service-quality interventions; a market with weak electricity and broadband access faces a more fundamental infrastructure constraint.

This distinction can improve how development programmes and investment priorities are framed.

05 · Limitations

What the dashboard does not prove.

  • The 11 countries are a selected comparison group, not a complete representation of Africa.
  • Indicator availability varies by country and year.
  • Country-level aggregates can conceal regional, rural and socioeconomic differences.
  • The relationships shown require further research before causal claims are made.
