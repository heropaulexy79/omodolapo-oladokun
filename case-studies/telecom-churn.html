Telecom Customer Churn Analysis

Which customers were most likely to leave, which factors were associated with attrition and where could management intervene to improve retention?

Problem Approach Findings Risk profile Recommendations

01 · The problem

More than one in four customers had left.

Customer churn reduces recurring revenue and raises the acquisition pressure required to replace lost customers. The analysis broke attrition into contract, billing, tenure and service factors so management could see where retention effort might be most useful.

7,043Total customers
1,869Churned customers
26.5%Overall churn rate

02 · The approach

Segment the rate until an actionable pattern appears.

Twenty-one customer, service and billing variables were analysed. Rather than stopping at the overall churn percentage, the project compared attrition across contract types, monthly charges, payment methods, customer tenure and support-service adoption.

03 · Key findings

The strongest signals were commercially actionable.

Contract and tenure

Month-to-month customers recorded the highest churn, while early-tenure customers were particularly vulnerable.

Charges and payments

Higher monthly charges were associated with stronger churn patterns, especially among month-to-month customers. Electronic Check users also recorded the highest churn among payment methods.

Support adoption

Customers without Technical Support and related value-added services churned more frequently, suggesting a possible connection between experienced value, problem resolution and retention.

Important caveatThese variables are associated with churn in the dataset. Payment method or support status should not be treated as a proven standalone cause.

04 · High-risk profile

A practical group for proactive monitoring.

The strongest combined risk pattern was:

New customer + Month-to-month contract + High monthly charges + Electronic Check + No Technical SupportThis profile gives the business a more focused group for testing retention interventions before cancellation occurs.

05 · Recommendations

Prioritise controllable points in the customer journey.

  • Test suitable incentives for profitable month-to-month customers to adopt longer-term contracts.
  • Review the value delivered by high-charge packages instead of assuming price alone is the problem.
  • Strengthen onboarding and proactive support during the early customer lifecycle.
  • Test whether improving support-service adoption changes retention outcomes.
  • Encourage lower-friction payment options as part of a broader programme, then measure the result.
